## Dependency notes

Strictly sequential — each story assumes the previous one is merged.

- **01 → 02.** Story 02 builds on the backend layering, the `get_db` session dependency, and the Vite/React shell created in Story 01.
- **02 → 03.** Story 03 depends on Story 02 for concrete shared contracts, not just convention:
  - `tickets.customer_id` is a FK to `customers.id` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and `Customer` gains a `tickets` relationship.
  - `create_ticket` reuses `_require_active` from `app/services/customers.py` to refuse tickets on archived customers.
  - `TicketDetailRead` embeds `CustomerRead` from `app/schemas/customer.py`.
  - The Alembic chain is linear: `0002_ticket_management` sets `down_revision = '0001'`.
  - The frontend reuses `api/client.ts` (`request`, `buildQuery`), `components/ui.tsx`, and the router introduced in Story 02, and adds a **Tickets** tab to `CustomerDetailPage`.
- **03 → 04.** Story 04 is the *foundation only* of Communication Channels (data model, driver interface, ticket-thread wiring); the five provider adapters are follow-up stories. It depends on Story 03 for concrete contracts:
  - `channel_messages.ticket_id` is a FK to `tickets.id` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and `customer_id` is denormalised off the ticket.
  - `app/services/channels/service.py` reuses `get_ticket` from `app/services/tickets.py` for its 404 semantics.
  - The Alembic chain stays linear: `0003_communication_channels` sets `down_revision = '0002'`.
  - The frontend adds a **Messages** tab to `TicketDetailPage` alongside Workflow and History.
- **01–04 → 05.** Story 05 is the first agent-facing story, and depends on all four:
  - The `agents` table from Story 03 is reused as-is, **not** recreated. `0004` only hangs `agent_tasks`, `quick_replies`, `ticket_notes`, `activity_events` and `activity_event_mentions` off it, and `down_revision = '0003'` keeps the chain linear.
  - `list_my_queue` reuses `priority_rank()`, extracted from `list_tickets` in `app/services/tickets.py`, so both order priority through the same CASE.
  - `app/services/tickets.py` and `app/services/channels/service.py` call `activity.record(...)` after assignment, status change and reply. Each takes a new **optional** `actor_agent_id` keyword, so both routes stay callable without agent context.
  - The frontend reuses `api/client.ts` (which now forwards `X-Agent-Id`), `components/ui.tsx`, and adds a **Notes** tab to `TicketDetailPage`.
- **01–03 → 06.** Story 06 (Customer Portal) depends only on Stories 01–03, not on 04 or 05 — it does not touch `channels`, `agent_tasks`, `quick_replies`, or `activity_events`. It introduces the **first real, credential-based principal type** in the codebase (`PortalUser` + `PortalSession`, bcrypt-hashed passwords, SHA-256-hashed bearer session tokens), deliberately distinct from the `Agent`/`X-Agent-Id` placeholder:
  - `portal_users.customer_id` is a FK to `customers.id` with `ON DELETE CASCADE`, and `Customer` gains a `portal_users` relationship; a `Customer` may hold many `PortalUser` logins.
  - `ticket_feedback.ticket_id` is a FK to `tickets.id` with `ON DELETE CASCADE` and a **unique constraint**, so feedback is upserted, not duplicated; `Ticket` gains a `feedback` relationship.
  - `app/api/deps_portal.py` reuses `_require_active`'s reasoning (not the function itself) to gate every portal request live against `Customer.is_archived`.
  - `app/services/portal.py` wraps `create_ticket`/`get_ticket`/`list_customer_tickets` from `app/services/tickets.py` rather than duplicating them, and reuses `Ticket.is_terminal` as the feedback-eligibility gate.
  - The Alembic chain stays linear: `0005_customer_portal` sets `down_revision = '0004'`, and introduces no new named enum types (unlike `0001`–`0004`).
  - The frontend adds a `/portal/*` route subtree (`PortalApp.tsx`) that never renders the agent `<Nav>`, with its own `portalAuth.ts`/`portalClient.ts` parallel to (not shared with) `agentContext.ts`/`client.ts`.
- **01, 03, 05 → 07.** Story 07 (Knowledge Base) is not part of the strict 01→06 chain, but has real technical dependencies, not just borrowed conventions:
  - `kb_articles.author_agent_id` is a nullable FK to `agents.id`; the `agents` table itself was introduced by Story 03 (`app/models/ticket.py`), not by Story 05.
  - The staff-side `/api/kb/...` router depends on `get_current_agent`/`CurrentAgent` from `backend/app/api/deps.py`, introduced by Story 05.
  - It does **not** depend on Story 04 (`channels`) or Story 06 (`portal_users`/`portal_sessions`) — the public `/api/portal/kb/...` endpoints are unauthenticated and never call `deps_portal.py`; Story 06 is referenced only as a router-structure precedent (`portal.py`), not a runtime dependency.
  - New tables use the same `_pk()` uuid-primary-key pattern as every table since Story 02 (`app/models/customer.py`), and the Alembic chain stays linear: `0006` sets `down_revision = '0005'`.
- **03, 04 → 08.** Story 08 (AI Foundation) is backend-only infrastructure — no route, no schema, no frontend. It depends on Story 03 for the `Agent`/`Ticket`/`TicketCategory` domain objects its `AIProvider` Protocol methods take as arguments, and on Story 04 for the `ChannelDriver` Protocol pattern (`app/services/channels/driver.py`) its own `AIProvider` Protocol is explicitly modeled on. It introduces `app/services/ai/provider.py` (`AIProvider`, `get_ai_provider()`, `AnthropicProvider`, `StubAIProvider`) and `app/services/ai/errors.py` (`AIProviderError`), plus `anthropic_api_key`/`ai_model`/`ai_enabled` settings — every one of Stories 09-13 imports from here rather than redefining any of it. `get_ai_provider()` is a plain, zero-argument module-level function, **not** a FastAPI dependency: the service layer stays FastAPI-free by this codebase's existing convention (`app/api/deps.py:10-12`), and tests swap providers via `monkeypatch.setattr` on the imported `app.services.ai.provider` module, not `application.dependency_overrides[...]`.
- **03, 08 → 09.** Story 09 (Ticket Summaries) adds `ai_summary`/`ai_summary_generated_at` to `tickets` and a new `ai_summary_generated` value to the pre-existing `ticket_event_type` Postgres enum (`0007_ai_ticket_summaries.py`, `down_revision = '0006'`) — the first migration in this arc to widen that enum, which has no Postgres-side reversal (`downgrade()` only ever drops the two columns). It is also the first story to let `AIProviderError` propagate out of a route, so it registers the `AIProviderError` → 502 handler in `backend/app/main.py` that Stories 11 and 13 both reuse rather than re-registering.
- **04, 08 → 10.** Story 10 (Suggested Replies) adds no schema and no new route file of its own significance beyond a shared `backend/app/api/routes/ai.py` (introduced here, extended by later AI stories rather than each adding a sibling router) — a suggestion is generated on demand and never persisted. It depends on Story 04's `list_messages_for_ticket` for thread context and Story 08 for the provider. Unlike Story 09, a provider failure here degrades locally to `StubAIProvider` output rather than surfacing a 502 — a suggested reply is optional and always human-reviewed before sending, so silently degrading is the better UX than an error banner.
- **03, 08, 09 → 11.** Story 11 (Automatic Categorization) adds `tickets.ai_suggested_category_id` (nullable FK to `ticket_categories.id`, `ON DELETE SET NULL`) and an `ai_category_suggested` enum value (`0008_ai_categorization.py`, `down_revision = '0007'`) — the first migration in the whole project to `op.add_column` an existing table rather than only `op.create_table`. A second FK from `tickets` to `ticket_categories` forces both `Ticket.category` and the new `Ticket.ai_suggested_category` relationships to declare explicit `foreign_keys=[...]`, or SQLAlchemy raises `AmbiguousForeignKeysError` at import time. Its on-demand recompute route reuses Story 09's `AIProviderError` → 502 registration in `main.py` rather than adding a second one — hence the dependency on 09, not just 08.
- **03, 07, 08 → 12.** Story 12 (Suggested Solutions) adds no schema — suggestions are computed live on every request from Story 07's `list_public_articles` (published-only) and are never persisted. The AI provider, when enabled, only re-ranks among those real rows; its output is validated against the real candidate id set before anything is returned, so a hallucinated id can never reach the response.
- **03, 06, 07, 08 → 13.** Story 13 (AI Chatbot) adds `chatbot_sessions`/`chatbot_messages` (`0009_ai_chatbot.py`, `down_revision = '0008'`), a new model file `app/models/chatbot.py` (not folded into `portal.py`), and a new router `app/api/routes/portal_chat.py` gated by Story 06's `get_current_portal_user`. It grounds every turn in Story 07's published KB search plus the customer's own open tickets (via Story 06's portal ticket listing), and follows this plan's own documented "a cross-customer lookup on a portal route returns 404, not 403" contract for a `session_id` belonging to another portal user. Story 08's `AIProvider.chat` signature (`chat(self, message: str, history: Sequence[Mapping[str, str]], *, context: str | None = None) -> str`) was finalized specifically to support this story's need for free-text grounding context instead of a single-`Ticket` parameter — see Story 08's own Prerequisites for that reconciliation.
- **03, 04, 05, 06 → 14.** Story 14 (Reports & Management) is pure read-only aggregation over data every one of those four stories already produces — it adds no table and no Alembic migration, the first story in the whole plan to do so. It depends on Story 03 for `Ticket`/`TicketEvent` and the `priority_rank()`/`OPEN_STATUSES` helpers in `app/services/tickets.py`; on Story 04 for `ChannelMessage.direction`/`created_at`, from which a ticket's first-response time is derived at query time (an outbound message's earliest `created_at`) rather than a stored column, so the shared `enqueue_outbound` send path is never touched; on Story 05 for the pure-aggregation-function-over-`Session` pattern established by `app/services/agent_dashboard.py`; and on Story 06 for `TicketFeedback`, which it aggregates for customer-satisfaction reporting rather than introducing a second, duplicate rating table. SLA targets are fixed per-priority module-level constants in `app/services/reports.py`, the same "tuning constant, not a config table" precedent as `activity.MENTION_WINDOW_DAYS` and `tickets.MAX_ESCALATION_LEVEL` — there is no admin-editable SLA policy in this story.
- **03, 06 → 15.** Story 15 (Staff Authentication, Users & Roles) is the "future auth story" this overview's own "Known cross-story gap" section (below) called for. It extends `Agent` (`app/models/ticket.py`, introduced by Story 03) **in place** — adding `password_hash`/`role_id` rather than introducing a separate `users` table — so `tickets.assignee_id` and every other existing FK to `agents.id` needs no migration at all. It depends on Story 06 for the entire mechanism it ports to the staff side: `PortalUser`/`PortalSession`'s bcrypt-hashed password + SHA-256-hashed bearer-token session pattern (`app/services/portal.py`, `app/api/deps_portal.py`) is copied almost line for line into a new `AgentSession` table and a rewritten `app/api/deps.py::get_current_agent`, which keeps its existing name and `Annotated` alias shapes (`CurrentAgent`/`OptionalAgent`/`CurrentAgentId`) so no route in Stories 05/08-14 needs to change. Ships with zero permission enforcement — every authenticated agent can do everything any agent could do before, plus call the new user/role-management endpoints this story adds.
- **15 → 16.** Story 16 (Permissions & Access Control) adds the `permissions`/`role_permissions` tables and a `require_permission(key)` dependency, then applies it retroactively to Story 15's own `/roles` and `/agents/{id}/set-password` endpoints — the first real restriction on what an authenticated agent may do. It deliberately does **not** gate the pre-existing, still-open `customers.router`/`tickets.router`/`channels.router`; see this file's "Known cross-story gap" section (rewritten by this story) for why.
- **15, 16 → 17.** Story 17 (Audit Logs) adds `audit_log_entries`, mirroring `app.services.activity`'s own append-only, same-transaction `record()` discipline. It depends on Story 15 for a real agent id to attribute actions to and Story 16 for the `view_audit_log` permission key (already present in Story 16's seeded catalog; this story is the first to wire a route that checks it). Every mutation Story 15/16 introduced (login, logout, password set, role created/updated, role assigned) gains an `audit.record(...)` call as part of this story.
- **16, 17 → 18.** Story 18 (System Configuration) is the first story in the whole plan to introduce a DB-backed, admin-editable settings table (`system_settings`) — a deliberate, explicitly-scoped departure from the "tuning constant, not a config table" precedent (`activity.MENTION_WINDOW_DAYS`, `tickets.MAX_ESCALATION_LEVEL`, `reports.SLA_*_MINUTES`) and from `app.core.config.Settings`'s env-only fields, neither of which this story touches or migrates. It depends on Story 16 for the `manage_system_config` permission and Story 17 for `audit.record()`, which every config write calls. This is the final story in the Security & Administration arc.

## Shared contracts to respect

- **Service layer never commits.** `get_db` (`backend/app/db/session.py` lines 30–43) owns the transaction; services `flush()` only.
- **Error mapping is centralised.** `NotFound`/`Conflict`/`PayloadTooLarge` → 404/409/413 in `backend/app/main.py` lines 33–35. Add new failure modes as `Conflict`, not as new exception types or per-route `HTTPException`s.
- **Schema changes go in Alembic**, never in `infra/postgres/init.sql`, which stays empty by design.
- **Named enums must be dropped explicitly in `downgrade()`** — `op.drop_table` leaves Postgres enum types behind and breaks the next `upgrade`. See `0001_customer_management.py` lines 116–120 for the pattern.
- **Conditional indexes must set both `postgresql_where` and `sqlite_where`**, or the SQLite-backed test suite enforces a different constraint than production.
- **Seeded reference data needs both paths.** A migration seed alone is invisible to the test suite, which builds its schema with `metadata.create_all`. Story 04 pairs the `0003` seed with an `after_create` hook on `Channel.__table__` and hard-coded row ids, so both paths produce identical rows. Alembic builds its own `Table` object, so the hook never fires during a migration.
- **Migrations are Postgres-only.** They emit `server_default=text('now()')`, which SQLite has no function for — harmless for DDL, fatal for a migration that INSERTs. Supply such values explicitly in a seed.
- **Ordering columns must be stamped in Python, not by the server.** SQLite's `CURRENT_TIMESTAMP` has second resolution, so rows written in one request tie and the order falls to the random uuid4 primary key. `ticket_events.created_at` and `channel_messages.created_at` both set `default=` for this reason.
- **A cross-customer lookup on a portal route returns 404, not 403.** This is a deliberate, documented exception to `Forbidden`'s own docstring reasoning (`backend/app/services/errors.py:18-25`), which justifies 403 only for internal, trusted-staff callers. On the public portal surface, "not yours" and "does not exist" must be indistinguishable, or the response leaks the existence of another customer's record. Story 06 established this; any future public-facing route must follow it too. Story 13's `ChatbotSession` lookups follow it as well.
- **Every AI capability (Stories 09-13) must degrade to `StubAIProvider` output when `settings.ai_enabled` is `False` or `settings.anthropic_api_key` is unset.** No story may make real Anthropic reachability a hard dependency for tests or local dev — `get_ai_provider()` (Story 08) is the single place that decision is made; no caller re-checks the settings itself.
- **`get_ai_provider()` is a plain module-level function, not a FastAPI dependency, and callers import the module, not the bare name.** `from app.services.ai import provider as ai_provider`, then `ai_provider.get_ai_provider()` — this is what lets `monkeypatch.setattr(ai_provider, "get_ai_provider", fake_factory)` take effect in tests. A bare `from app.services.ai.provider import get_ai_provider` import binds the function at import time and will not observe a monkeypatch. (Story 13's route layer is the one place that also wraps it in `Depends(get_ai_provider)` to inject it into its service function as an explicit parameter — a valid variant of the same underlying plain function, not a contradiction of this rule.)
- **`AIProviderError` (`app.services.ai.errors`, re-exported from `app.services.ai.provider`) is never mapped centrally.** Story 08 registers no handler for it in `backend/app/main.py` and leaves the decision to each caller: a best-effort path (ticket creation, a suggested reply) catches it and degrades silently; an explicit user-triggered action that has nothing else at stake (regenerate a summary, recompute a category) lets it propagate to a clean, deliberately-registered `502`. Story 09 registers that one `AIProviderError` → 502 handler in `main.py`; Story 11 and Story 13's route layers reuse it rather than re-registering.
- **AI-generated content must be visually labeled as AI-generated at its point of display, everywhere it appears** — a ticket summary, a drafted reply, a suggested category, a suggested KB article, a chatbot message. This applies to `StubAIProvider` output too, since the UI has no reliable way to distinguish a stub answer from a real one and must not try to guess.
- **No AI output is ever auto-applied without an explicit user action.** No auto-sent reply, no auto-applied category, no ticket created or modified by the chatbot on a customer's behalf — every one of Stories 09-13 routes the actual mutation (if any) through an existing, already-reviewed endpoint (`PATCH /tickets/{id}`, the existing send-message flow, `POST /portal/tickets`), never a new AI-specific write path.

## Known cross-story gap

**Updated by Stories 15-16.** Staff authentication is now real: Story 15 replaced the `X-Agent-Id` trusted-header placeholder (`backend/app/api/deps.py`, `frontend/src/api/agentContext.ts` — both retired) with bcrypt-hashed passwords and SHA-256-hashed bearer-token sessions (`agent_sessions`), extending `Agent` (Story 03) in place rather than introducing a separate `users` table — so `tickets.assignee_id` and every other existing FK to `agents.id` needed no migration. Story 16 added a real role/permission system (`roles`, `permissions`, `role_permissions`) and a `require_permission(key)` dependency. `PortalUser`/`PortalSession` (Story 06) remains a structurally parallel but intentionally **separate** identity scheme for customers — staff and customers are different trust domains with different data-access rules, and unifying the two logins was not attempted.

**Deliberately still open, by explicit choice documented in Story 16, not oversight:**

- `customers.router`/`tickets.router`/`channels.router` (aside from its inbound-webhook route, which must stay open for external providers) remain unauthenticated. Gating them would require rewriting most of `backend/tests/conftest.py`'s test fixtures (the plain, unauthenticated `client` fixture underpins the `customer`/`ticket`/`ticket_category` setup fixtures nearly every existing test file depends on) — a distinct, mechanical migration effort out of proportion to the Security & Administration intake, left to a future story.
- `Interaction.author`/`TicketEvent.actor` remain free text, not a real FK to `agents.id` — unrelated to authentication and out of scope for Stories 15-18.
- `unread_mentions`, still approximated as "mentions in the last 7 days" (`activity.MENTION_WINDOW_DAYS`), because there is still no per-agent read cursor — unrelated to authentication/authorization.
- Agents still have no timezone. `tasks_due_today` counts a UTC day while the frontend renders local time, so the two can disagree either side of midnight — unrelated to authentication/authorization.
